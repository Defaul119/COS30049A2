\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We built and evaluated a pipeline that ranks a Bitcoin address's transactions by how unusual each is for that
address. Our answers:
\begin{enumerate}[label=RQ\arabic*]
  \item The robust z-score baseline is the Explorer's method: it is within the margin of the best method on DS1,
    stable, explainable, and the most consistent on unseen addresses.
  \item Supervised classifiers do well on Elliptic until behaviour changes, and stay weak on BitcoinHeist across
    time and on unseen families.
  \item Unusual activity and the labelled illicit class barely overlap, so a flag is a reason to look, not a
    verdict.
  \item TxGPT, trained only on other addresses, ranks first on the held-out DS10; the Assistant explains
    flags faithfully under a guardrail.
\end{enumerate}

The most significant insight is that methods tuned on one sample of addresses lose much of their advantage on
another, so an independent held-out test was essential. Future work for Assignment~3 includes showing TxGPT's
surprisal beside the baseline, more addresses, graph models for labelled data, and a usability study with the
intended users.
